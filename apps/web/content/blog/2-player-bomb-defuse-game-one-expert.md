A 2 player bomb defuse game works well. Bomb Appétit needs at least two people: one Defuser who sees the bomb and one Expert who holds the manual. With a single Expert, that person does every lookup alone, so a pair plays at a steadier pace and both players hear every word of every exchange.

## Does a 2 player bomb defuse game work with one Expert?

Yes. Two is the smallest group Bomb Appétit supports, with one Defuser and one Expert. Every mission, the manual and online rooms all work for a pair. The difference from a bigger group is that one Expert carries the whole manual, so nobody shares the lookups and nobody argues about which rule applies.

The game was built for two to five players: one Defuser and one to four Experts. Two people sit at the bottom of that range, and nothing is switched off for them. The bomb looks the same, the manual reads the same and the timer is just as unforgiving.

What changes is the conversation. With three or four Experts, a group splits the manual and often has several people talking at once. With one Expert, every instruction comes from one voice and every question goes to one person. That makes a pair a good way to learn the game. The Defuser hears one clear instruction at a time, and the Expert gets to know every page instead of a few.

It also changes the load. A lone Expert has to find each module's section, read its rules and keep the edgework notes in view, all while the Defuser waits. On a bomb with one or two modules, that is comfortable. On a bomb with six, the Expert is the bottleneck, which is why the mission choice below matters for a pair.

Many groups looking for party games for 2 people want something that is not head to head. A bomb defusal round fits that: the two players are on the same side, and the opponent is the clock. If the pair signs in, a defusal can also appear on the mission's leaderboard, where the Expert is named beside the Defuser.

If you are new to the roles, the walkthrough of [how one round of a bomb defusal game runs](/blog/bomb-defusal-game-how-a-round-works) covers the start, the middle and the moment the bomb goes quiet.

## Setting up a 2 player bomb defuse game on two devices

A 2 player bomb defuse game needs two devices, or one device and a printed manual. The Defuser opens the bomb on the larger screen. The Expert opens the manual on the other. The two check that the rule seed matches, turn the bomb away from the Expert, and start talking. Apart, the pair joins an online room instead.

### In the same room

1. The Defuser opens [Play](/play) and picks a mission. For a first game, that is First bite.
2. The Expert opens the [standard manual](/manual/1) on a phone, tablet or second computer, or uses a printed copy.
3. Both read out the rule seed. The bomb and the manual must show the same number. Seed 1 is the standard.
4. The Defuser turns the bomb screen so the Expert cannot see it. Sitting across a table, facing each other, makes this easy.
5. The Defuser starts the bomb.

A laptop for the bomb and a phone for the manual is the most common pair setup. The bomb is easiest to handle on a bigger screen, and the manual reads well on anything. The full comparison of setups, including two phones and a printed manual, is in the guide to [what a free bomb defusal game needs before the first round](/blog/bomb-defusal-game-free-what-you-need).

### Far apart

1. One player, the host, opens Play and creates a room.
2. The host sends the five-letter room code to the other player.
3. The other player joins with that code.
4. One player picks Defuser and the other picks Expert.
5. The pair starts a phone or video call, and then the host starts the bomb.

The game has no voice chat built in, so the call is part of the setup. In an online room, the Expert's screen shows the time left, the strike count and how many modules are solved, but never the bomb. A pair who wants to play over a video call on phones can follow the steps for [setting up a FaceTime call and a bomb on two devices](/blog/games-to-play-on-facetime-with-friends#setting-up-a-facetime-call-and-a-bomb-on-two-devices).

## Pacing for a lone Expert: who flips the pages?

With two players, the Expert flips every page. Nobody else can look anything up, and the Defuser is not allowed to read the manual. The pair goes faster when the Defuser names the module before describing it, so the Expert can open the right section while the details are still coming.

The manual is organized to make this workable. It opens with a short section on how to use it, then gives each module its own section, then an appendix on edgework and a glossary at the end. A lone Expert who knows that order can jump straight to a module by name.

A few habits keep the lone Expert from falling behind:

- **Write the edgework down at the start.** The manual says to collect the serial number, batteries, indicators and ports once and note them. A solo Expert cannot afford to ask for them again in the middle of a module.
- **Ask for the module name first.** "Which module?" before "What does it look like?" lets the Expert open the page while the Defuser describes.
- **Say where you are.** "On the Big Button page" tells the Defuser that the lookup has started and that silence is the Expert reading, not the Expert lost.
- **Finish one module before opening the next.** Two half-solved modules mean two pages to hold in mind, and a lone Expert has no partner to hand one to.

The Defuser has a pacing job too. While the Expert reads, the Defuser can call out the time, check the back of the case for more modules, or keep an eye on a needy module. What the Defuser should not do is fill the silence with guesses, because each guess pulls the Expert off the page.

With three or more players, the manual can be split so each Expert owns a few modules. That changes the pacing completely, and the guide to [splitting the manual between two Experts](/blog/games-for-3-players-one-defuser-two-experts#splitting-the-manual-between-two-experts) explains how a bigger group divides it.

## Missions that fit a pair, and ones to leave for later

The first missions fit a pair best: First bite, Button up and Strange symbols each give five minutes for one to three modules. A lone Expert can learn each new page without the clock taking over. Missions with many modules, needy modules or a reduced strike limit can wait until the pair talks smoothly.

All three missions below use rule seed 1 and the default limit of three strikes.

| Mission         | Time limit | Modules                             | Why it suits two players                                                                                                                |
| --------------- | ---------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| First bite      | 5 minutes  | 1 (Wires)                           | One page for the Expert to learn. The pair practises the describe, instruct, repeat loop with no other modules competing for attention. |
| Button up       | 5 minutes  | 2 (Wires, Big Button)               | Adds the first decision between tapping and holding. The Expert has two pages, a manageable number to flip between alone.               |
| Strange symbols | 5 minutes  | 3 (Wires, Big Button, Glyph Keypad) | Brings in glyphs, so the pair has to agree on names for symbols. With one Expert, there is only one person to agree with.               |

After those three, the first section continues with Table for three and Quick snack, which shorten the timer to four minutes. Both stay within the same three modules, so a pair can try them once the first three feel easy.

Some missions are better left for later. Rush hour puts six modules on a six-minute bomb, and its own description suggests splitting the manual between Experts, which a pair cannot do. Pressure cooker brings the first needy module, which means the lone Expert has to answer interruptions while working through another page. The last missions ask the most: Double shift has fourteen modules and three needy ones on a bigger case, and the final mission, Bomb Appétit, allows just one strike with a manual from a different rule seed.

A pair can still play all of these. The point is the order. The full [mission list](/missions) shows what each step adds, so a pair can choose the next challenge deliberately rather than jumping to the hardest bomb.

## The two-player mistake: the Expert reading ahead

The common two-player mistake is the Expert reading ahead: picking a rule before the Defuser has finished describing the module. With nobody else to slow things down, a lone Expert jumps to the first rule that looks right. Wait for the full description, then read the matching list from the top.

Wires shows why. The manual has a separate list for each number of wires, from three to six. If the Defuser says "wires, red, blue..." and the Expert starts reading the four-wire list, the answer is only right if there turn out to be exactly four wires. The Expert should wait for the count, then go to the matching list and stop at the first line that is true.

Big Button is another trap. The rule list asks about the button's color, the word on it, the battery count and sometimes a lit indicator. An Expert who hears "a blue button" and reads ahead may give an answer before hearing the word on the button, and the word can change the result.

In a bigger group, a second Expert often catches this, because they are listening while the first is reading. In a pair, nobody is. The fix is a simple routine: the Expert asks "Is that everything on the module?" before reading a single rule, and the Defuser answers "That's everything" when the description is complete.

The Defuser's part of the routine is the repeat-back. When the instruction comes, the Defuser says it back in full before touching the bomb. If the Expert read ahead and picked the wrong list, saying it back gives both of them one more chance to notice. The [how to play page](/how-to-play) lists this among its tips, and it is the single habit that costs a pair the least and saves them the most.
