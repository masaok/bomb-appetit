Team building activities for remote teams can cost nothing. A free browser game, the call tool your team already uses and a written rota are enough for an hour of real cooperation. In a bomb defusal round, one person sees the bomb, the others hold the manual, and the rota makes sure every teammate takes the bomb once.

## Why team building activities for remote teams rarely need a budget

Team building activities for remote teams rarely need a budget because the expensive parts of an in-person offsite, the venue, the travel and the catering, do not exist on a call. What a distributed team needs is a shared task, a reason to talk and a clear structure. Those come from the activity's design, not from spending.

Bomb Appétit covers the task for free. It is a co-op bomb defusal game that runs in a browser, with no install and no account. Guests can play every mission. Signing in with GitHub only adds leaderboards and saved progress, so nobody on the team has to create a login to take part. The code is open source under the MIT license, which also means there is no trial period waiting to run out.

The call is the second piece, and your team already pays for it or uses a free one. The game has no voice chat of its own on purpose. Players talk over the same call they use for standups, which removes one more thing to install and test.

The third piece is a rota. It costs nothing but ten minutes of the organizer's time, and it is the part that decides whether the hour works. Without one, a free activity still wastes an hour if the same few people do all the playing.

What remains is hardware people already have. The bomb is easiest on a laptop or tablet, and the manual reads fine on a phone. Any recent version of Chrome, Firefox, Safari or Edge runs both.

## An hour of team building activities for remote teams

An hour of team building activities for remote teams works best as three rounds of rising difficulty, each in small rooms, with the whole team together at the start and the end. Spend about ten minutes on setup, about forty on three rounds with role changes, and the last ten on a group debrief.

Small rooms matter because one bomb holds two to five people: one Defuser and up to four Experts. A team of eight cannot share one bomb, and taking turns while four people watch leaves half the team idle. Splitting into two or three rooms keeps everyone in a role every round.

Each room needs its own audio. If your call tool can split people into smaller groups, use that. If not, each room can run a separate call and everyone returns to the main one between rounds. The host of each room creates it from Play and shares a five-letter code. If your team has never used room codes before, the explanation of [how a room code connects browsers far apart](/blog/multiplayer-browser-games-to-play-with-friends#how-a-room-code-connects-browsers-far-apart) is a short read to send in advance.

Pick missions that grow with the team's confidence. Strange symbols is a good first round: three modules on a five-minute timer, and the first appearance of Glyph Keypad, where players have to agree on names for odd symbols. Quick snack works for a final round, with four modules and only four minutes. Both draw their bombs at random, so a room that plays a mission twice faces a fresh case each time.

Short on time? The [thirty-minute version for one room](/blog/virtual-team-building-games-everyone-talks#running-a-virtual-team-building-round-in-thirty-minutes) runs two rounds and a debrief, which suits a team of five or fewer. For a lighter start to a regular meeting, the guide to [virtual team building games that get the whole call talking](/blog/virtual-team-building-games-everyone-talks) explains why this format draws in quiet people.

## How do you rotate roles so quiet people get the bomb?

Rotate roles so quiet people get the bomb by writing the rota before the session and giving every person exactly one turn as Defuser. Put quieter colleagues in the Expert seat first, with one manual page each, so they learn the rules before taking the bomb in round two or three. Publish the rota so nobody has to ask.

The Defuser is the role that sees the bomb and touches it, and it is the most exposed seat. An Expert holds part of the manual and gives instructions without seeing the bomb. Giving each Expert a named part of the manual matters as much as rotating the Defuser, because "everyone reads everything" lets the fastest reader do all the work.

Here is an example rota for a team of eight across three rounds. The names are examples. Rounds 1 and 2 use three rooms on Strange symbols, whose modules are Wires, Big Button and Glyph Keypad. Round 3 uses two rooms of four on Quick snack.

| Round | Room | Defuser | Experts and their manual pages                                 |
| ----- | ---- | ------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | A    | Ana     | Ben: Wires and edgework. Chloe: Big Button and Glyph Keypad.   |
| 1     | B    | Dev     | Ema: Wires and edgework. Femi: Big Button and Glyph Keypad.    |
| 1     | C    | Gus     | Hana: all three module pages and edgework.                     |
| 2     | A    | Ben     | Dev: Wires and edgework. Hana: Big Button and Glyph Keypad.    |
| 2     | B    | Ema     | Gus: Wires and edgework. Ana: Big Button and Glyph Keypad.     |
| 2     | C    | Chloe   | Femi: all three module pages and edgework.                     |
| 3     | A    | Femi    | Ana: Wires. Ben: Big Button. Gus: Glyph Keypad and edgework.   |
| 3     | B    | Hana    | Chloe: Wires. Dev: Big Button. Ema: Glyph Keypad and edgework. |

Every person defuses once. Femi and Hana, the two people in this example who asked to go last, each read the whole manual alone in a pair room before their turn, so they reach the bomb having seen every rule. Room C in rounds 1 and 2 is a pair, one Defuser and one Expert, which is slower but calm.

Mix the rooms between rounds. Nobody in the example sits with the same Defuser twice, so each person hears several ways of describing a bomb. That matters for the debrief, where people compare what worked.

## How do you run the session across time zones?

Run the session across time zones by choosing the shortest overlap that everyone can attend, cutting the hour to what fits, or splitting the team into two regional sessions that play the same missions. The game needs every player in a room live at once, so there is no way to play one bomb across a gap of several hours.

If the overlap is thirty minutes, run one round per room and a short debrief instead of three rounds. Keep the rota and carry it to the next session, so the people who did not defuse this time go first next time.

If there is no overlap, two sessions can still feel like one event. Each region plays the same missions on the same day. People who sign in with GitHub appear on the [mission leaderboards](/leaderboard), where each mission has its own board and every listed run has been replayed by the server to check it. The Experts are named beside the Defuser. Afterwards, a short shared post comparing the two regions' rounds gives the team something to talk about together.

Signing in stays optional. Anyone who prefers not to can play as a guest. Their rounds still count for the session, and they simply do not appear on a board.

Whichever shape you pick, send the start time in each person's local time rather than one central zone. A time like "16:00 Lisbon, 11:00 Toronto" leaves nobody doing the conversion in their head.

## The remote session mistake: no rota, so the same three people play

Running a session with no rota is the most common remote mistake, because the same three people end up playing every round. Without a plan, whoever answers "who wants the bomb?" fastest takes it. The confident volunteers defuse, everyone else reads or watches, and the quiet half of the team leaves having done what they do in every meeting.

It happens even with good intentions. The organizer asks for volunteers to keep things relaxed. The volunteers are, naturally, the people most comfortable speaking up. By round three the pattern is set, and asking a quiet colleague to take the bomb in front of a watching group feels like putting them on the spot.

The rota fixes this before the session starts. When everyone can see that round two has their name in the Defuser column, there is nothing to volunteer for and nothing to dodge. It also lets people prepare. Someone nervous about defusing can read the [How to play page](/how-to-play) the day before.

A second symptom is the Expert who reads every page. If the rota gives one person all three module pages in a room of four, the other two Experts have nothing to do. Assign pages per person, as in the example table, and stick to it during the round.

## Remote team building session plan

The remote team building session plan below covers a one-hour session for a team of eight, using the rota above. It lists the timing, what the organizer does, and what everyone else does. Times are an example; the organizer can stretch the setup block if people need help joining a room.

| Time (example) | Organizer                                                                                     | Everyone else                                                               |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Day before     | Send the rota, the link to the site and a note that no account or install is needed.          | Read the rota and check a browser on their device.                          |
| 0:00 to 10:00  | Explain the two roles. Open the rooms for round 1 and confirm each Defuser has posted a code. | Join their room and open the manual at rule seed 1 on their assigned pages. |
| 10:00 to 22:00 | Visit each room's audio in turn and keep time.                                                | Round 1 on Strange symbols.                                                 |
| 22:00 to 34:00 | Call the swap, then move people to their round 2 rooms.                                       | Round 2 on Strange symbols, with new Defusers and new pages.                |
| 34:00 to 48:00 | Call the swap and set up the two larger rooms.                                                | Round 3 on Quick snack.                                                     |
| 48:00 to 60:00 | Bring everyone back to the main call and run the debrief.                                     | Answer two or three questions about what happened and what to change.       |

The debrief is where the hour turns into something the team uses. A set of questions for it, with examples of useful answers, is in the guide to [debriefing team building communication games](/blog/team-building-communication-games-bomb-debrief).

Keep the plan in a shared document during the session so anyone who drops off the call can see where to rejoin.
