Good games to play on FaceTime with friends need talking, not a shared view, and a bomb defusal round is built on exactly that. Keep the FaceTime call on your phone, open the bomb or the manual in a browser on a laptop. One friend defuses, the others read the rules, and nobody points a camera at anything.

## Why a bomb round suits games to play on FaceTime with friends

A bomb round suits FaceTime because the game only works if players cannot see each other's screens. FaceTime is a call between faces, and that is all the game asks of it. The Defuser describes the bomb, the friends with the manual answer, and the phone carries the conversation without sharing anything else.

Many games feel awkward on a phone call. Card and board games need everyone to see the same table, so somebody ends up holding a phone over a board while the others squint. A bomb defusal game reverses that. In Bomb Appétit, one player sees the bomb and the rest have only the manual, so not seeing is the rule, not the obstacle.

The group size fits FaceTime too. The game takes two to five players: one Defuser and one to four Experts. A pair can play on an ordinary one-to-one call, and a bigger group can use a Group FaceTime call. Rounds are short, with the first mission giving five minutes for one module, so a round fits into a call that started for some other reason.

It also costs nothing extra. The game is free, runs in any recent browser on a computer, tablet or phone, and needs no account. Friends who have never heard of it can be playing within a few minutes of the call starting. Signing in with GitHub is only for leaderboards and saved progress, and nobody on the call needs it.

## Setting up a FaceTime call and a bomb on two devices

The setup is one device for the call and one for the game. Start the FaceTime call on each phone, then open the game in a browser on a laptop or tablet. The host creates an online room there, reads or texts the five-letter code to the others, and each friend joins and picks a role.

The order for the host:

1. Start the FaceTime call and check everyone can hear.
2. On a laptop, open [Play](/play) and create a room.
3. Send the five-letter room code to the group by text message, so nobody mishears a letter.
4. Each friend opens the game on their own laptop or tablet, joins with the code and picks Defuser or Expert. There is one Defuser per bomb.
5. Prop the phones up so faces stay visible, then the host starts the bomb.

How the room code links the browsers, and what each screen shows while friends join, is explained in [how a room code connects browsers far apart](/blog/multiplayer-browser-games-to-play-with-friends#how-a-room-code-connects-browsers-far-apart). For FaceTime, the only extra detail is that the code travels by text, not by voice.

Not every friend has the same gear. This table rates the common combinations for each role. The verdicts are our recommendations, based on the game's note that the bomb is easiest on a bigger screen and the manual reads well on anything.

| Devices                                  | As the Defuser                                                       | As an Expert                                           | Verdict                                       |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| Phone for the call, laptop for the game  | Best: the bomb gets a large screen and the call stays out of the way | Very good: a big manual page and free hands            | The setup to aim for                          |
| Phone for the call, tablet for the game  | Good: a tablet is big enough for the modules                         | Very good: the manual is easy to scroll and read aloud | A fine choice for anyone without a laptop     |
| One phone for both the call and the game | Poor: the bomb is cramped and the call keeps leaving the screen      | Workable: the manual reads well on a phone             | Use only for an Expert, never for the Defuser |

The Defuser should always get the best screen in the group. Modules have small parts, and some need holding or careful taps. An Expert can manage with less.

## Can the Expert read the manual on the same phone as the call?

An Expert can read the manual on the same phone as the FaceTime call, but it is a compromise. Apple's guide on how to [use other apps during a FaceTime call on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/use-other-apps-during-a-call-iph3dc0cb20d/ios) says you can open another app mid-call and keep talking. That lets an Expert switch to the browser and read.

The trade-off is attention. While the manual fills the phone, the Expert loses sight of the Defuser's face and has to tap the green bar at the top of the screen to get back to the call, as Apple's guide describes. In a slow round that is fine. In a round with a needy module waking up, every trip away costs a second.

There are ways to make it easier. Open the manual before the call starts, so the browser tab is ready. Use the manual's contents to jump to a module's page instead of scrolling. If two Experts are on the call, give the one with only a phone a small share of the modules, such as Wires and the Big Button, and give the rest to the Expert with a laptop.

Paper is another way out. The manual prints cleanly in black and white from the browser's print command. An Expert who has only a phone can keep the call on screen and read from printed pages. Before the round, check that the rule seed printed on the paper matches the one the room shows. The standard manual is [rule seed 1](/manual/1).

The Defuser should not try this. The bomb needs the full screen and the Defuser's full attention, and leaving the call mid-module means losing the Experts' faces when a quick nod would help.

## What breaks the audio on FaceTime, and what fixes it?

Most FaceTime audio trouble in a bomb round comes from sound looping between devices: the laptop's game sounds or a second device in the same room getting picked up by a phone's microphone. Headphones on the phone fix most of it. A quiet room and one call device per room fix the rest.

The common problems, and what to do about each:

### Echo from two phones in one room

When two friends in the same house both join the call on separate phones, each phone hears the other's speaker. The result is an echo that makes every instruction hard to follow. Put one phone on the call for that room, or give both people headphones.

### Game sounds leaking into the call

Bomb Appétit plays sound effects. On the Defuser's laptop, those sounds come out of the speakers and into the phone's microphone, so the Experts hear a muffled copy over the call. Either lower the laptop volume a little or have the Defuser wear headphones connected to the phone. The Experts can rely on their status line for the timer and strikes, so they lose nothing.

### Two people talking at once

When the Defuser and an Expert speak over each other, neither instruction lands, and on a phone call there is no table to lean across. Headphones on the Defuser help, because the Experts' voices arrive clearly. The bigger fix is a habit: one instruction at a time, then the Defuser repeats it back before acting.

### A weak connection

A shaky signal makes words drop out. The game itself is less affected, because each person's browser runs its own view. Move closer to the Wi-Fi router, and if the video stutters, it is fine to turn cameras off for a round. Voices matter more than faces here.

## The FaceTime mistake: pointing the camera at the bomb

The most tempting FaceTime mistake is the Defuser flipping the phone's camera round to show the bomb on the laptop. It feels helpful when a symbol is hard to describe, but it hands the Experts the one view the game is built to deny them. Keep the camera on faces and describe instead.

FaceTime makes this slip easy. The camera switch is one tap, and Apple's guide notes that you can even share your screen in a FaceTime call. Both moves turn the round into a picture quiz, where the Experts stop asking questions and start studying a wobbly image of a laptop.

A smaller version is a Defuser who angles the phone so the laptop screen is just in shot behind them. Check the background before the round starts.

If a symbol truly resists description, the answer is a name. Agree on "the curly one" or "the ladder" and keep using it. The how-to-play guide calls this agreeing on words, and it is one of the [communication tips](/how-to-play) that make later rounds faster.

If the camera does slip onto the bomb, stop the round and start again. A first mission takes five minutes, so a restart costs little.
